# Verb audit and reader review list — November 2026

The rebuilt book contains **635 full verb tables**, **334 compact separable
entries**, and **969 entries in each verb index**. Its adjective reference has
**60 adjectives in two tables side by side** (printed page 5, PDF page 9).

Page numbers below refer to the numbers printed in the rebuilt book. The PDF
viewer page number is four higher because of the front matter. This list
separates corrections from editorial clarification; a changed record does not
necessarily mean its earlier conjugation was wrong.

## Usage and meaning clarifications

These changes improve the learner reference; they are not all corrections of
ungrammatical word forms.

| Table and printed page | Clarification |
| --- | --- |
| **sich ereignen** (p. 39); **geschehen** (p. 122); **gelingen** (p. 120); **sich lohnen** (p. 45); **erfordern** (p. 99); **betragen** (p. 79); **dauern** (p. 88); **passieren** (p. 158); **fließen** (p. 113) | Show the normal third-person uses for the meanings given, instead of mechanically filling ich/du/wir/ihr and commands. Dashes mean “not normally used in this meaning”, not that a literary personification is impossible. |
| **kosten** (p. 141) | Restored all six finite persons: a person can be the subject of costing someone something, as in **ich koste dich nichts**. No ordinary imperative is shown for the cost sense. |
| **hängen** (p. 126) | The existing **hing/gehangen** and **hängte/gehängt** forms were valid but lacked a meaning distinction. Construction rows now distinguish a hanging state from hanging something up. |
| **backen** (p. 63); **melken** (p. 152); **saugen** (p. 172); **verwenden** (p. 228) | Labels now acknowledge variable patterns. Selected displayed forms remain valid; alternative or historical forms are not automatically errors. |
| **bringen** (p. 87); **nennen** (p. 154); **verbringen** (p. 213) | Standardised the broad “irregular” label to the more informative **mixed**. “Irregular” was not itself false. |

| **nützen** (p. 155) | Clarified “benefit” (dative) alongside regional transitive “use” (accusative); the background now represents both cases. |
| **kontrollieren** (p. 140); **leisten** (p. 146); **lösen** (p. 149); **merken** (p. 152); **sich ziehen** (p. 57); **erscheinen** (p. 104) | Clarified glosses: check/monitor; perform/achieve; solve/loosen/release; notice; drag on/pull oneself; appear/seem to someone. Reflexive afford/remember meanings are not implied by the bare non-reflexive tables. |
| **bringen** (p. 87); **eröffnen** (p. 104); **finden** (p. 112); **schaden** (p. 172); **schließen** (p. 177); **reklamieren** (p. 168); **vereinbaren** (p. 214); **strecken** (p. 193); **trösten** (p. 201); **melken** (p. 152) | Removed misleading construction glosses or replaced underspecified rows with clearer ordinary patterns. For example, **schaden + Dat** replaces a row whose English translation incorrectly reversed who was harmed. |

Full **-ere** forms, such as **ändere**, **fordere**, **liefere** and
**vergrößere**, replace contracted forms throughout the relevant tables.
This is a consistency choice, not a claim that every contraction was wrong.
The simple past now uses **schliefst**, **trankst** and **schrien**; the
imperative of **sitzen** is shown as **sitz!**. These choices avoid unnecessary
rare or competing forms in an everyday reference.

## How the check was performed

This section records the linguistic audit before the later user-directed
restoration of `ahmen`. The original historical weak forms are restored from
the earlier source, with an explicit obsolete label; `nachahmen` is now a
compact entry rather than a full table. The current totals appear above.

1. Reviewed the full catalogue inventory: conjugations, compound tenses,
   classifications, glosses, constructions and compact lists. The earlier
   object-colour audit is recorded separately in `verb-case-colours.md`.
2. Compared the person forms, participles and infinitives against available
   Duden conjugation tables. Structured tables were obtained for **617 of the
   635 entries**. This includes ambiguous lookup results requiring manual
   selection; the count is not a claim that 617 unambiguous automatic matches
   establish correctness.
3. Reviewed the remaining 18 against dictionary descriptions and regular or
   documented strong paradigms: **sich freuen, sich fürchten, sich regen,
   sich sorgen, sich verlaufen, sich wundern, betragen, gelingen, nützen,
   pflegen, regen, saugen, schaffen (create), schaffen (manage), spazieren
   gehen, vergrößern, verlängern, weitergeben**.
4. Manually resolved lookup/variant differences for **backen, heißen,
   senden, verwenden, wachsen, wenden, wiegen, zweifeln**. In particular,
   **heißte/geheißt** belongs to hoisting, not being called; **wiegte/gewiegt**
   belongs to rocking, not weighing. Those lookup results did not cause
   correct book forms to be overwritten.
5. Checked all full-table wir/formal imperatives against the applicable
   finite/reflexive/prefix pattern. Retained the intentional negative
   **verletzt euch nicht!** examples.
6. Added validation for agreement between heading and perfect auxiliaries,
   correct past auxiliaries, identical perfect/pluperfect participles,
   matching future infinitives, reflexive flags, and compact split spelling.
   Added content regressions for the confirmed participle, prefix, imperative
   and meaning distinctions. **All 13 tests pass.**
7. Rebuilt the **260-page PDF**. Both indexes contain **968 entries** with
   no rejected entries. The final LaTeX log has no overfull/underfull boxes or
   warnings. Text bounds were checked across every page, and representative
   corrected tables and the adjective layout were visually inspected.

A schema test cannot prove that a German form or translation is correct.
Neither a dictionary's limited conjugation display nor an automatic mismatch
is sufficient on its own. The checks above describe the actual review;
they are not a guarantee that a large reference can never contain an error.
